01 · How to use it

Four steps, one board seat

Read it before you accept a role, and again before each board meeting. Mark every check red, amber or green as you go; your marks and notes stay in this browser and add up in the scorecard at the end.

1

Know your seat

Advisor, NED, chair or fractional executive carry very different accountability. Start there.

2

Place the company

Where it sits in the regulated value chain, which box of the fintech map, B2C or B2B, and who owns it.

3

Work its sub-sector

Each sub-sector has its own checks. Ask the questions, request the board-pack evidence, watch for the red flags.

4

Score and follow up

Use the scorecard to see where the gaps are, and take the open questions to the next meeting.

“The key question for board members is always, where does it sit in the regulated value chain?”
Susanne Chishti, Chair and Founder, FINTECH Circle

02 · Know your seat

Four roles, four levels of accountability

Choose the role you will enjoy and feel comfortable with, knowing exactly what it makes you answer for.

RoleStatusWhat it meansWatch
Fractional CXOExecutiveA part-time executive (for example a CFO one day a week, or a chief risk or compliance officer two days a week). Responsible for delivery, with executive authority, embedded in management.Not a “board light” role. Do not underestimate the time and risk involved.
Advisory board memberNon-executive, not a directorNo direct management oversight, no formal fiduciary duty, influence only. Can be paid a fixed fee plus equity or options.If the advice is ignored, there is little to be done. Bring advice a CEO cannot get quickly from an LLM.
Non-executive directorDirectorListed at Companies House. In regulated firms answerable to regulators (FCA, PRA). Clear fiduciary duties and formal oversight.As liable as an executive director, while working perhaps three days a month.
ChairDirector, leads the boardChairs the board and is responsible for the board’s effectiveness.Sets how the board works, not only what it decides.

UK regulatory accountability: four points

1. Personal accountability
In a regulated firm you answer personally, not only collectively.
2. The regulatory perimeter
Even an unregulated technology company usually operates inside a regulated value chain.
3. Oversight that fits the firm
Board oversight must reflect the firm’s operational and compliance requirements.
4. D&O insurance
Directors’ and officers’ cover should be in place for NEDs, chairs and advisers.

Who owns it changes what the board is for

OwnershipWhat to expectThe board’s core question
Founder-led / earlyFounder influence is strong; you may be one of the first experienced governance voices.Is the board building governance that can scale with the company?
VC-backedMinority stakes; scaling and market share over near-term profit; few independent NEDs, some appointed by the VC.Are we building the right product, and is it being scaled? (Product–market fit.)
PE-backedLater stage, often profitable; majority stakes; focus on EBITDA, execution, forecasting, sometimes leverage.Is execution stable, growing and profitable, and is the financial structure sound?
PublicDetailed regular disclosure, institutional investors, more independents, nomination, remuneration, audit and risk committees.Are reporting, committees and market communication disciplined enough for scrutiny?

VC and PE owners both seek an exit, typically over 3 to 7 years and sometimes up to 10.

Source: Board Programme, Module 3, sections 5 and 6.

03 · Read the business

Place the company before you judge it

Fintech is technology-enabled financial services and infrastructure, delivered through a digital operating model, usually in a regulated or quasi-regulated setting.

Where does it sit in the regulated value chain?

01Front-end apps and distributionOwns the customer relationship and the journey.
02Product manufacturerCreates the regulated product: credit, loans, insurance, investment products.
03Infrastructure enablementProcessing, APIs and compliance tooling that others build on.
04Data and identity layerCustomer verification and transaction monitoring.

Which box of the fintech map?

Consumer or business; on balance sheet or capital light; with an infrastructure layer underneath. Fintechs have targeted every box.

Consumer · balance sheetConsumer lending, deposit takingFintech: Neobanks, buy now pay later, digital mortgages, crypto custody
Consumer · capital lightData aggregation, advice, comparisonFintech: Robo-advisers, budgeting tools, digital wealth managers, pension dashboards
Business · balance sheetSME and business lending, trade financeFintech: Invoice financing, revenue-based financing, supply chain finance, embedded lending
Business · capital lightSaaS tools, B2B payment orchestrationFintech: Expense management, B2B payments, payroll, treasury and accountancy tools, regtech
Infrastructure layerPayment rails, banking as a service, APIs, enabling platformsFintech: Card issuing, BaaS, open banking APIs, KYC/AML and onboarding, core banking SaaS

B2C, B2B or hybrid?

ModelCharacterRevenue drivers
B2CFast growth, high marketing spend, more fragile unit economics.Transaction fees, interest margin, subscription tiers, referral and partner economics.
B2BSlower, longer enterprise cycles; recurring revenue; higher switching costs.SaaS subscriptions, usage-based pricing, enterprise licensing and support.
HybridBoth revenue drivers, but more complex to govern.A mix of the two; watch which one the plan really depends on.
Source: Board Programme, Module 3, section 1.

04 · The numbers that matter

Revenue quality, not just revenue

“It’s not enough just to grow revenue but the quality of the revenue is key.”
Susanne Chishti, Module 3
B2C: avoid the growth illusion
  • CAC and payback period
  • Lifetime value and retention
  • Active use and the timing of churn
  • Revenue diversification
B2B: prove the recurring model
  • ARR
  • Net revenue retention
  • Contract length
  • Renewal rates (ideally renewed before expiry)
  • Top-client concentration
  • Success of implementations and pilots
The valuation test
Is the valuation driven by fundamentals and real value, or by narrative? Companies with a good story but weak fundamentals have been repriced.
Revenue quality
Margins, churn, and any dependence on unsustainable incentives. Since the post-2022 reset, runway, profitability and risk maturity matter as much as growth.

Unit economics as a governance tool

Boards watch the high-level funnel, not day-to-day sales. Strong products need strong distribution: land, then expand.

Generate the right leadsAwarenessSelectionOnboardingGrow existing customersAdd new customers

Sub-sector 1 of 8

Paytech

Merchant acquiring, card issuing and processing, gateways, cross-border payments and FX.

01
Why it matters. Payments is the largest fintech revenue pool in the UK and Europe, which makes control failures expensive and visible.

1. Fraud and financial controls

Ask
  • Who owns fraud risk at executive level, and how often does the board see it?
  • How are fraud losses and chargebacks trending against appetite?
In the board pack
  • Fraud and loss dashboard against risk appetite
  • Results of the latest controls review or internal audit

2. Operational resilience and incident response

Ask
  • Which services must never stop, and what is the tolerance for disruption on each?
  • When did we last rehearse a major outage, and what changed afterwards?
In the board pack
  • Important business services and impact tolerances
  • Incident log with root causes and actions closed

3. Regulatory perimeter and partner dependencies

Ask
  • Which licences do we hold, and which do we rely on partners for?
  • What happens to customers if a key sponsor bank or scheme partner exits?
In the board pack
  • Map of licences and critical partners
  • Exit or contingency plan for each critical partner

4. Revenue concentration and platform risk

Ask
  • How much revenue comes from the largest merchants or one platform?
  • What would a change in a scheme’s or platform’s terms do to margin?
In the board pack
  • Revenue by top clients and channels
  • Margin sensitivity to fee or scheme changes
Red flags
  • Fraud reported only as a total, never against appetite
  • Incidents closed without a root cause
  • One partner the firm cannot operate without, and no plan
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 2 of 8

Lending and alternative credit

SME and consumer lending, buy now pay later, short-duration credit.

02
Why it matters. “Often rapid growth can hide latent losses if credit scoring technologies have not been implemented well.” (Susanne Chishti, Module 3)

1. Underwriting discipline

Ask
  • Has the credit model been validated independently, and when?
  • Who can override a decision, and how often does it happen?
In the board pack
  • Model validation report
  • Override volumes and outcomes

2. Risk appetite

Ask
  • Is credit risk appetite written down, and does the board approve changes to it?
  • Where are we closest to a limit today?
In the board pack
  • Board-approved risk appetite statement
  • Limits dashboard with headroom

3. Portfolio monitoring and early warning signs

Ask
  • Which early warning indicators reach the board, and how quickly?
  • How are newer loan vintages performing against older ones?
In the board pack
  • Arrears and default by vintage
  • Early warning indicator pack

4. Funding model and liquidity

Ask
  • How concentrated is our funding, and when do facilities renew?
  • What happens to lending if a funder pulls back?
In the board pack
  • Funding mix and maturity profile
  • Liquidity forecast with downside case

5. Stress testing across cycles

Ask
  • How much loss can we absorb in a severe downturn?
  • Do the stress scenarios reflect a full credit cycle, not just recent data?
In the board pack
  • Stress test results and loss absorption
  • Scenario assumptions approved by the board
Red flags
  • Growth targets set before risk appetite
  • Loss data only from a benign period
  • A single funding line behind most of the book
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 3 of 8

Embedded finance

Financial products built into non-financial platforms, such as shopping, e-commerce and marketplaces, via APIs, open banking and partners.

03
Why it matters. Several legal entities share one customer journey, so accountability has to be explicit.

1. Accountability in multi-party ecosystems

Ask
  • For each product, which entity is accountable for the customer outcome?
  • Is that written into the contracts, or only assumed?
In the board pack
  • Responsibility map by product and entity
  • Key contract terms on accountability

2. The customer understands the journey

Ask
  • Can a customer tell whose product they are buying and who to complain to?
  • What do complaints say about confusion in the journey?
In the board pack
  • Customer journey review
  • Complaints themes and volumes

3. Hand-offs between legal entities

Ask
  • Where does the customer pass from one entity to another, and what can fail there?
  • Who monitors those hand-offs?
In the board pack
  • Hand-off points with control owners
  • Failure and exception reporting

4. Data sharing and consent

Ask
  • Is consent captured and recorded at every point where data is shared?
  • How would we evidence that to a regulator?
In the board pack
  • Consent and data-sharing register
  • Privacy and data protection review

5. Resilience across dependencies

Ask
  • Which outside dependency would stop the product if it failed?
  • Have we tested failover with partners, not just internally?
In the board pack
  • Dependency map
  • Joint resilience test results
Red flags
  • “The partner handles that” with no contract to show it
  • Complaints routed between entities with no owner
  • Consent assumed rather than recorded
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 4 of 8

Digital assets and DeFi

Crypto assets and tokenised value on distributed ledgers: cryptocurrencies, stablecoins linked to fiat, and DeFi. Spans payments, capital markets and infrastructure.

04
Why it matters. Rules differ by jurisdiction; in Europe, MiCA gives a harmonised framework.

1. Regulatory perimeter and jurisdiction (e.g. MiCA)

Ask
  • Which activities are regulated in each market we serve, and under which regime?
  • What is our plan where the rules are still changing?
In the board pack
  • Regulatory map by jurisdiction and activity
  • Regulatory change tracker

2. Custody and safeguarding of client assets

Ask
  • Where are client assets held, and are they segregated from our own?
  • Who holds the keys, and how is access controlled?
In the board pack
  • Custody and safeguarding arrangements
  • Asset reconciliation results

3. Market integrity and partners

Ask
  • How do we detect manipulation or abuse on our platform?
  • How were exchanges, custodians and liquidity partners vetted?
In the board pack
  • Market surveillance reporting
  • Partner due diligence

4. Operational resilience

Ask
  • What is the plan for a hack, a key loss or a chain outage?
  • How quickly could we notify customers and regulators?
In the board pack
  • Incident and recovery plan
  • Security testing results

5. Conduct risk and ecosystem accountability

Ask
  • Do customers understand the risks of the products we offer?
  • Who is accountable when a third party in the ecosystem fails?
In the board pack
  • Financial promotions review
  • Responsibility map across the ecosystem
Red flags
  • Client and company assets held together
  • No clear answer to “who holds the keys”
  • Serving a market before its rules are mapped
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 5 of 8

Wealthtech

Digital brokerages, investment platforms, robo-advice, managed platforms, AI-powered equity research.

05
Why it matters. Growth rides on digital distribution, retail participation and market cycles, so conduct and disclosures carry the risk.

1. Conduct risk and suitability

Ask
  • How do we assess a client’s risk at onboarding?
  • How do we know products are suitable for the clients who hold them?
In the board pack
  • Suitability and onboarding review
  • Conduct risk indicators

2. Market cycle sensitivity

Ask
  • What happens to revenue and client behaviour in a falling market?
  • Which controls stop a client taking risk they cannot bear?
In the board pack
  • Revenue sensitivity to market levels
  • Controls testing results

3. Clear customer communication and disclosures

Ask
  • Are disclosures clear across voice, website and app, especially for complex products?
  • How do we identify and support vulnerable customers?
In the board pack
  • Communications review
  • Vulnerable customer policy and outcomes

4. Governance of product design (UK Consumer Duty)

Ask
  • How does the board see evidence of good customer outcomes under the Consumer Duty?
  • Who signs off a new product, and against what?
In the board pack
  • Consumer Duty outcomes report
  • Product approval process and recent decisions
Red flags
  • Growth reported without outcome data
  • Complex products sold through a simple app journey with no extra checks
  • No view of vulnerable customers
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 6 of 8

Regtech

Onboarding, AML and KYC, regulatory reporting, ID verification and screening.

06
Why it matters. Demand is driven by regulatory complexity and rising compliance cost; new regulation can speed up sales cycles.

1. Deep risk and compliance expertise

Ask
  • Does the board have the risk and compliance depth our clients expect?
  • Who on the board can challenge the product’s regulatory logic?
In the board pack
  • Board skills matrix
  • Regulatory expertise in the team

2. Model risk and explainability

Ask
  • Can we explain to a client’s regulator how the model reached a decision?
  • How are models monitored for drift and bias?
In the board pack
  • Model inventory and validation
  • Explainability documentation

3. Data governance and privacy

Ask
  • What data do we process for clients, where is it held, and who can access it?
  • When were data controls last tested?
In the board pack
  • Data governance framework
  • Privacy and security audit results

4. Liability across the value chain

Ask
  • If our tool misses something, where does liability sit: with us or the client?
  • Do our contracts and insurance match that answer?
In the board pack
  • Contract liability terms
  • Insurance cover summary
Red flags
  • A model nobody outside the team can explain
  • Liability terms that differ from client to client with no view of the total
  • Sales plan that depends on one regulation arriving on time
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 7 of 8

Insurtech

App and online distribution, underwriting automation, claims technology. Can enable incumbents or run a hybrid B2C and B2B model.

07
Why it matters. The customer judges an insurer at the claim, so outcomes and partners matter as much as growth.

1. Underwriting governance

Ask
  • Who approves changes to pricing and underwriting rules?
  • How do we know automated underwriting is working as intended?
In the board pack
  • Underwriting authority and change log
  • Loss ratio by product

2. Claims experience and customer outcomes

Ask
  • Are claims outcomes acceptable to customers, and how do we know?
  • What do complaints and declined claims tell us?
In the board pack
  • Claims outcome reporting
  • Complaints and declined-claims analysis

3. Reputation risk

Ask
  • What would a public claims failure do to the brand and to partners?
  • How quickly would the board hear about it?
In the board pack
  • Reputation risk register
  • Escalation route to the board

4. Partner dependencies

Ask
  • Have underwriting and capital partners been tested and verified?
  • What if a capacity provider withdraws?
In the board pack
  • Partner due diligence
  • Capacity contingency plan

5. Operational resilience and fraud risk

Ask
  • Could we keep paying claims through a systems outage?
  • How is claims fraud detected and reported?
In the board pack
  • Resilience testing results
  • Fraud reporting
Red flags
  • Claims speed reported, but not claims outcomes
  • Capacity from one provider with no fallback
  • Pricing changes made without a clear approval
Board checks: Board Programme, Module 3, section 2. Questions, board-pack evidence and red flags: FINTECH Circle.

Sub-sector 8 of 8

Capital markets and institutional fintech

Trading and execution infrastructure, risk analytics, decision tools, treasury workflow.

08
Why it matters. “To what extent do you put all your eggs in one basket … you want to make sure that you diversify your client base.” (Susanne Chishti, Module 3)

1. Long sales cycles

Ask
  • How many entry points and contacts do we have in each target institution?
  • Is the pipeline balanced with smaller, faster customers?
In the board pack
  • Pipeline by stage and age
  • Sales cycle length trend

2. Client concentration

Ask
  • What share of revenue sits with the largest client or two?
  • What is the plan if one of them leaves?
In the board pack
  • Revenue by top clients
  • Renewal dates and risk

3. Resilience

Ask
  • What uptime have we promised, and what have we delivered?
  • How are incidents handled and reported to clients?
In the board pack
  • Uptime against SLAs
  • Incident reports

4. Implementation risk at go-live

Ask
  • Which large client go-lives are coming, and what could delay them?
  • Who owns implementation risk at executive level?
In the board pack
  • Implementation plan and status
  • Pilot and go-live success record
Red flags
  • The first large client is most of the revenue
  • Go-live dates that keep moving
  • SLAs promised without the data to prove them

13 · Worksheet

Before your first board meeting

Ten questions for any fintech board, whatever the sub-sector. Write your answer, or the name of who can give it.

  1. 01Where does the company sit in the regulated value chain?
  2. 02Who owns it, and what does that owner expect from the board?
  3. 03Is the valuation driven by fundamentals or by narrative?
  4. 04How good is the revenue: margins, churn, any dependence on unsustainable incentives?
  5. 05Which three metrics tell the board whether the model works?
  6. 06Which licences, partners and dependencies could stop the business?
  7. 07How does the board hear about a serious incident, and how fast?
  8. 08Is D&O cover in place, and does it cover my role?
  9. 09Where is governance behind the company’s stage, and how do we close it without killing innovation?
  10. 10What do I bring that management cannot get elsewhere?
“Fintech companies scale ups have to act like grown up sooner and that’s again where governance comes in.”
Susanne Chishti, Module 3
